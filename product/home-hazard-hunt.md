# The 20-Minute Home Hazard Hunt™

## Find Every Tip-Over, Fall, and Leak Risk in Your Home — In One Pass

**Why this worksheet exists:** The single biggest myth about earthquakes is that the
building is the danger. In the vast majority of quake injuries, the actual cause is a
falling bookshelf, a toppled water heater, shattered glass, or an unsecured TV — not
structural collapse. You cannot fix what you haven't found. This audit finds every
hazard in your home in a single 20-minute room-by-room walk.

**How to run it:** Print this sheet (or open it on your phone). Walk each room in
order below and check off every item you find. Don't fix anything during the hunt —
just find and mark. Then execute the anchoring/fixes this week, prioritizing the
items marked "HIGH."

**The master rule:** If it is tall, heavy, glass, or connected to gas/water and it
is NOT secured, it is a hazard. Full stop.

---

## Zone 1 — Kitchen (4 minutes)

**Checklist — mark everything you find, note priority:**
- [ ] [HIGH] Refrigerator not strapped/anchored (can tip forward in strong shaking)
- [ ] [HIGH] Overhead cabinets without child-safety or seismic latches (contents fly out)
- [ ] Heavy items stored on top shelves (move to lower shelves — gravity is the enemy)
- [ ] Glassware/dishes in cabinets without latches (add rubber bumpers or latches)
- [ ] Knife block or knives on open counters near edges
- [ ] Hanging pot racks not secured to a joist
- [ ] Gas stove/range not secured with a flexible gas line and anti-tip bracket
- [ ] Heavy small appliances (stand mixer, toaster oven) near counter edges
- [ ] Wine/liquor bottles on open shelving

## Zone 2 — Living Room & Family Room (4 minutes)

**Checklist:**
- [ ] [HIGH] TV not secured with an anti-tip strap or wall mount
- [ ] [HIGH] Bookshelves/tall furniture not anchored to wall studs
- [ ] [HIGH] Glass-front cabinets or china cabinets not secured/latched
- [ ] Mirrors and heavy art not on closed hooks (use closed hooks, not open ones)
- [ ] Freestanding lamps in walkways (can fall and block exits)
- [ ] Fireplace items (poker sets, glass doors) not secured
- [ ] Heavy decor on high shelves (move down or secure)
- [ ] Electronics/media units not anchored if tall/top-heavy
- [ ] Ceiling fans / light fixtures with visible loose mounting

## Zone 3 — Bedrooms (4 minutes, repeat per bedroom)

**Checklist:**
- [ ] [HIGH] Tall dressers/wardrobes not anchored (documented cause of child injuries
      — dressers tip onto beds)
- [ ] [HIGH] Bed not positioned away from windows and tall furniture that could fall on it
- [ ] Bookshelves not anchored
- [ ] Mirrors on closets/walls not on closed hooks
- [ ] Items on nightstands that could fall (lamps, glass items)
- [ ] Closet shelving overloaded with items that could fall when door opens
- [ ] Shoes/flashlight NOT kept by the bed (glass on the floor is a common post-quake
      injury — keep sturdy shoes and a flashlight within arm's reach of every bed)

**Zone 3 tally:** ______ hazards found (multiply by number of bedrooms audited)

## Zone 4 — Bathroom & Utility Areas (4 minutes)

**Checklist:**
- [ ] [HIGH] Water heater not strapped to wall studs (a top cause of house fires and
      floods after quakes — an unsecured tank can tip, rupture the gas line, and leak)
- [ ] [HIGH] Gas shutoff valve location UNKNOWN or wrench not stored nearby
      (locate it now — see Tool #6 for the exact shutoff procedure)
- [ ] Medicine cabinet items not secured (glass bottles, razors on open shelves)
- [ ] Heavy mirror not on closed hooks
- [ ] Washer/dryer not secured if stacked (stacking kits should be bolted)
- [ ] Furnace/HVAC unit not secured or has flexible gas connector
- [ ] Electrical panel access blocked by stored items (keep clear for emergency access)
- [ ] Chemicals/cleaners stored on high, unsecured shelves (can spill/break, creating
      a hazardous mix)

## Zone 5 — Garage, Storage & Exterior (4 minutes)

**Checklist:**
- [ ] [HIGH] Garage storage shelving not anchored (heavy tools/paint cans fall)
- [ ] Chimney with visible cracks or loose masonry (have inspected — falling chimney
      material is a documented exterior hazard)
- [ ] Exterior gas meter area clear and accessible (no storage blocking it)
- [ ] Overhead garage storage racks overloaded or not rated for the load
- [ ] Tall freestanding shelving in garage not anchored
- [ ] Propane tanks / flammables not secured or stored per safety guidance
- [ ] Exterior walkway/path clear of items that could block egress after a quake
- [ ] Address numbers visible from the street (helps first responders find you fast)

## Pro Tips From the Field

1. **Anchor kits are cheap and fast.** L-brackets, museum putty, furniture straps,
   and flexible gas line kits cost under $50 total for a typical home and take a
   weekend to install. This is the highest-ROI prep action in the entire system.
2. **Photograph the "before."** Photos of unsecured items become your fix-it list
   and your proof of progress in two weeks.
3. **Do the bedrooms first if you have kids.** Tipped dressers onto beds are one of
   the most preventable and most common quake injuries to children.
4. **Re-hunt after any renovation or new furniture.** New bookshelf, new TV, new
   baby's room — re-run the relevant zone.
5. **Ask your landlord if you rent.** Water heater strapping and structural anchors
   may need landlord approval or a handyman — ask this week, not after the next quake.
