# The Family Emergency Plan™

## Lock In Your Meeting Points, Contacts, and Reconnect Protocol — Before You Need It

**Why this worksheet exists:** After a significant earthquake, phone networks jam,
family members may be scattered (school, work, errands), and panic makes decision-
making worse, not better. A written, rehearsed plan removes the guesswork exactly
when guessing is most dangerous. This is a fill-in-the-blanks worksheet — complete
it once, review it every 90 days, and post copies where every family member can see them.

**How to run it:** Fill in every blank as a family. Print at least 3 copies: one on
the fridge, one in the go-bag (Tool #3), and one in each family member's wallet/bag
as a wallet card (template at the bottom).

## Section 4 — The Communication Protocol

1. **Text first, call second.** Text messages queue and deliver even when voice
   networks are jammed; calls often fail to connect during high-traffic events.
2. **Check in with the out-of-area contact within the first hour if possible** —
   even a one-word "safe" text.
3. **Don't drive to check on people unless necessary.** Roads may be damaged or
   congested; a phone check-in is safer and faster than driving across town.
4. **Social media as backup.** If phone networks are fully down, a public "marking
   safe" post can reach people faster than a jammed network — but is not a
   replacement for the direct out-of-area contact check-in.

## Pro Tips From the Field

1. **Actually call your out-of-area contact and ask them.** Don't just write their
   name down — confirm they're willing and understand the role.
2. **Practice the text-check-in once, unannounced,** as part of your Drop-Cover-Hold
   drill (Tool #4) — send the real text to the real contact so everyone knows the flow.
3. **Update the plan every time something changes:** new school, new job, new pet,
   new phone number. Tie updates to your 12-Week Tracker (Tool #8) reviews.
4. **Laminate the fridge copy.** It needs to survive years of kitchen life to be
   there when you need it.
5. **Teach kids the plan in simple terms they can repeat back to you** — "if we're
   not together, go to the flagpole, and Aunt Jamie will know where everyone is."
