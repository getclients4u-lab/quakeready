# The Work / Car / Travel Playbook™

## The Correct Move Wherever You Are — Not Just At Home

**Why this worksheet exists:** Most earthquake prep focuses entirely on the home —
but you spend roughly half your waking hours somewhere else: the office, the car,
traveling, at the gym, in a store. This playbook covers the moves and prep for every
place you're likely to be, so the system travels with you.

**How to run it:** Read through each scenario once. Do the one-time prep items
(work bag, car kit tie-in) this week. Review before any trip.

---

## Scenario 1 — At Work / The Office

**One-time prep:**
- [ ] Know your building's emergency evacuation plan and meeting point (ask HR/
      facilities if you don't already know it)
- [ ] Keep a small personal kit at your desk: comfortable shoes (if you wear heels/
      dress shoes), a granola bar, a small flashlight, any daily medication
- [ ] Know where the stairwells are — do NOT plan to use elevators during/after

**During:** drop, cover, hold on under your desk or against an interior wall, away
from windows and tall filing cabinets/shelving.

**After:** follow your workplace's evacuation plan if instructed. Use the stairs.
Text your out-of-area contact and household once safely outside. Do not drive home
immediately if roads may be congested/damaged — assess first.

---

## Scenario 2 — In the Car

**One-time prep:** Your car kit from Tool #3, Section 7 — water, first aid, cash,
comfortable shoes, phone charger.

**During:**
- [ ] Pull over as quickly and safely as possible
- [ ] Avoid stopping under or on overpasses, bridges, near buildings, trees, or
      power lines
- [ ] Stop, put the car in park, keep your seatbelt on, and stay inside — the car's
      frame offers real protection from falling debris
- [ ] Turn on the radio for official updates while you wait

**After:** Check for damage to roads/bridges before continuing. Avoid downed power
lines and damaged infrastructure. If a bridge or overpass looks damaged in any way,
do not cross it — find another route or wait for official clearance.

---

## Scenario 3 — Public Transit (bus, train, subway)

**During:**
- [ ] Stay seated, brace against the seat in front of you, and cover your head/neck
- [ ] If standing, grab a stable handhold and crouch low
- [ ] Do not attempt to exit a moving vehicle

**After:** Follow the operator/conductor's instructions. If evacuation is ordered,
move calmly to the nearest safe exit, watching for gaps, debris, and other
passengers. Text your check-in as soon as you're safely off and clear of the area.

---

## Scenario 4 — Shopping / Retail / Public Buildings

**During:** drop, cover, hold on — get away from shelving units, glass display
cases, and heavy signage, which are common tip-over/fall hazards in retail spaces.
Get under a sturdy table/checkout counter if available, or against an interior wall.

**After:** follow store staff instructions for evacuation. Watch for fallen
merchandise and broken glass underfoot on your way out. Do not use elevators or
escalators.

---

## Scenario 5 — Gym / Pool / Recreational Facility

**During:** move away from windows, mirrors, and heavy equipment racks (weight
racks are a real tip-over risk). Drop and cover under something sturdy if available,
or against an interior wall. If in a pool, exit the water as quickly and calmly as
possible — pools can slosh violently (seiche effect) during shaking.

**After:** follow facility staff instructions. Check for broken glass (mirrors,
windows) before walking barefoot.

---

## Scenario 6 — Traveling / Hotel Stays

**One-time prep before any trip:**
- [ ] Note your hotel room's exit route (stairwell location) when you check in —
      make this a habit, every trip
- [ ] Pack a mini version of your go-bag essentials: flashlight, whistle, a day of
      water/snacks, any critical medication, a copy of your ID
- [ ] Share your itinerary with your out-of-area contact before you leave

**During:** same universal move — drop, cover, hold on, away from windows and tall
furniture (hotel dressers/TVs are often not anchored the way your own home now is).

**After:** use stairs, not elevators, to evacuate if instructed. Follow hotel staff
and local official instructions — you're less familiar with the area's specific
risks (tsunami zones, structural quality) than a local, so lean on local guidance.

---

## Scenario 7 — Coastal Areas (traveling or living)

**Additional rule beyond the universal move:** if you feel strong or prolonged
shaking while at or near the coast, **move to higher ground or inland immediately
after the shaking stops** — do not wait for an official tsunami warning if the quake
itself was strong; natural warning signs (a strong quake felt near the coast) come
before official alerts in many cases. Confirm safety with official sources once
you're on higher ground, not before moving.

## The One-Page Quick Card (cut out, keep in wallet/bag)

```
WHEREVER YOU ARE: DROP. COVER. HOLD ON. Away from windows/tall objects.
DRIVING: pull over away from overpasses/lines, stay in car, seatbelt on.
COASTAL: after shaking stops, move to higher ground immediately.
ALWAYS: text (don't call) your out-of-area contact once safe.
```

## Pro Tips From the Field

1. **The 5-second habit for any new place:** glance for the nearest sturdy table/
   desk and the nearest stairwell the moment you arrive somewhere. This becomes
   automatic with repetition and costs zero extra time.
2. **Frequent flyers/road warriors:** keep the travel mini-kit permanently packed
   in your travel bag so trip prep never skips this step.
3. **If you work in a high-rise, know the specific "shelter" floors/zones** your
   building has designated — many high-rises have specific interior refuge areas.
4. **Teach teenage drivers the car protocol explicitly** — "pull over away from
   overpasses" is not obvious to a new driver and is worth a specific conversation.
5. **Revisit this playbook before any trip to an unfamiliar seismic region** — risk
   awareness that's automatic at home needs a conscious refresh when traveling.
