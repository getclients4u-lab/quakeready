# The Drop, Cover, Hold On Drill™

## Train the 90-Second Reflex Until It's Automatic — For Every Location You'll Actually Be In

**Why this worksheet exists:** During an earthquake, you do not have time to think.
The households that come through with the least injury are the ones who had already
turned "drop, cover, hold on" into a physical reflex — trained, not just read about.
This tool gives you the exact move for every location you're likely to be in, plus a
real drill script to run with your family, timed, until it's automatic.

**How to run it:** Read the location-specific moves once. Then run the physical
drill with your household — actually get down on the floor, actually get under the
table, actually hold the position for 60 seconds. Do this quarterly (Tool #8 tracks it).

**The master rule, in five words: Drop. Cover. Hold on. Wherever.**

---

## Part 1 — The Universal Move (memorize this)

1. **DROP** to the ground, wherever you are, immediately — do not wait to see if it's
   "a big one," do not try to reach another room.
2. **COVER** your head and neck with your arms, and get under/against something
   sturdy: a sturdy table or desk, or against an interior wall away from windows,
   mirrors, and anything that could fall.
3. **HOLD ON** to whatever you're under (or hold your own head/neck) until the
   shaking completely stops. Expect it to feel long — most quakes last under 60
   seconds, but adrenaline makes it feel much longer.

**The three myths to unlearn immediately:**
- ❌ **"Run outside."** The leading cause of quake injury is being struck by falling
  or flying objects *inside*. You are safer dropping in place than sprinting for a door.
- ❌ **"Stand in a doorway."** Modern doorways are not stronger than the rest of the
  room, and standing in one exposes you to a swinging door and passing debris.
- ❌ **"The triangle of life" (lie next to furniture, not under it).** This theory is
  not supported by search-and-rescue data or emergency management agencies. Get
  UNDER sturdy furniture, or cover against an interior wall — official guidance,
  worldwide, is drop-cover-hold, not "triangle of life."

## Part 2 — Location-Specific Moves (know all of these)

### If you're indoors (home, office, school)
- Drop, get under a sturdy desk/table if one is within a step or two.
- If no sturdy furniture is near, drop against an interior wall, away from windows,
  mirrors, tall furniture, and anything hanging.
- Cover your head/neck with your arms either way. Hold on until shaking stops.

### If you're in bed
- **Stay in bed.** Grab a pillow to cover your head/neck and hold on. Getting up
  in the dark, disoriented, with glass potentially on the floor, is more dangerous
  than staying put — unless something heavy is positioned to fall directly on the bed
  (which is why Tool #2's bedroom audit matters).

### If you're outdoors
- Move away from buildings, trees, streetlights, and power lines if you can do so
  immediately and safely — falling facades and power lines are the outdoor risk.
- Drop in an open area and cover your head/neck. Hold on until shaking stops.

### If you're driving
- Pull over as quickly and safely as possible, avoiding overpasses, bridges,
  buildings, trees, and power lines.
- Stop the car, put it in park, and stay inside with your seatbelt on until the
  shaking stops — a car offers real protection from falling debris.

### If you're in a high-rise
- Same universal move: drop, cover, hold on, away from windows and exterior walls.
- **Do not use the elevator during or immediately after.** Expect fire alarms and
  sprinklers to activate — this is normal, not necessarily a fire.
- After shaking stops, use stairs to evacuate if instructed, checking each landing.

### If you're at the beach or near a coastline
- After the shaking stops, if you're in a coastal area, move to higher ground
  immediately — a strong coastal earthquake can generate a tsunami with very
  little warning. Do not wait for an official alert if the shaking was strong and
  you're near the shore; move inland/uphill first, confirm after.

### If you use a wheelchair or have limited mobility
- Lock the wheelchair brakes, stay seated, and cover your head/neck with your arms
  or whatever is available.
- If a companion is present, they should drop and cover near you rather than trying
  to move you during the shaking.

## Part 3 — The Family Drill Script (run this, out loud, timed)

**Setup (2 minutes):** Gather the household. Explain you're running a real drill,
not just talking about it. Assign one person as the "caller."

**The drill (repeat 3 times, different scenarios):**

1. Caller shouts: **"EARTHQUAKE! DROP, COVER, HOLD ON!"**
2. Everyone physically drops to the floor immediately, gets under/against the
   nearest sturdy furniture or interior wall, covers head/neck, and holds the
   position.
3. Caller starts a 60-second timer out loud: "Shaking... shaking... still shaking..."
4. At 60 seconds, caller says **"Shaking has stopped. Check yourself, then check
   your neighbor."**
5. Everyone does a real self-check (any pain? can you move?) then checks the person
   nearest them.
6. Caller says **"Move to the family meeting point."** Everyone physically walks
   there (see Tool #5 for your meeting point).

**Run this scenario 3 times, in 3 different rooms of the house**, so it's not tied
to one specific spot. Then run it once outdoors and once in the car if possible.

**Timing goal:** everyone should be down, covered, and holding within **3 seconds**
of the call. If it takes longer, run it again. This is the entire point of a drill —
building a reflex fast enough to matter.

---

## Part 4 — Special Notes for Kids

- Make the drill a game the first few times ("freeze like a turtle!") — younger
  kids respond better to a fun framing than a scary one.
- Practice the sound of a real earthquake early warning alert (many phone systems
  have a test/preview tone) so it's not a jump-scare the first time they hear it.
- Teach kids that if they're at school, they should follow their teacher's
  instructions — schools run their own drills, and consistency matters more than
  a different home-taught version.
- Reassure them explicitly: **"You know exactly what to do. That's why we practice."**
  Confidence reduces panic more than any single fact you can tell them.

## Pro Tips From the Field

1. **Drill without warning once a quarter.** A surprise drill (say, during dinner)
   tests the real reflex far better than an announced one.
2. **Drill in the dark once.** Power is often out after a real quake — knowing the
   floor plan blind matters.
3. **Update the drill as kids grow.** A toddler's "get under the table" becomes a
   teenager's full location-specific repertoire — re-teach as they age.
4. **Post the universal move somewhere visible** — a fridge magnet or laminated
   card by the door reinforces it between drills.
5. **The drill is the highest-ROI 15 minutes in this entire system.** Everything
   else prepares your supplies. This prepares your body's automatic response —
   and that's what actually happens in the first 90 seconds.
