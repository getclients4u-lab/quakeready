# The 72-Hour Go-Bag Checklist™

## Build a Grab-and-Go Kit for Every Person in Your Household — Plus the Car

**Why this worksheet exists:** After a significant earthquake, help may be delayed
for 72 hours or longer — stores empty fast, power and water can be out, and roads
may be blocked. A go-bag is the single highest-leverage prep action you can take,
and yet most "go bags" are really just an old backpack with a granola bar in it.
This checklist builds a real one, per person, done right the first time.

**How to run it:** Build one bag per household member (adjust the list for kids,
pets, and elderly/medical needs), plus one car kit. Check off each item as you
add it. Set a phone reminder to re-check expiration dates every 6 months
(Tool #8 automates this for you).

**The master rule:** Your bag should let one person survive 72 hours with zero
outside help — water, food, light, warmth, communication, and first aid, at minimum.

---

## Section 1 — Water & Food (per person)

- [ ] **Water: 1 gallon per person per day × 3 days** (3 gallons minimum per person)
      — store in food-grade containers, rotate every 6 months
- [ ] Water purification tablets or a small filter straw (backup if bottled runs out)
- [ ] 3 days of non-perishable, no-cook food per person (energy bars, dried fruit,
      nut butter, canned goods with a pop-top — no can opener needed)
- [ ] A manual can opener (if any canned food needs one)
- [ ] Salt/electrolyte packets (heat + stress = dehydration risk)

---

## Section 2 — Light, Power & Communication

- [ ] Flashlight (LED, per person) + spare batteries stored separately
- [ ] Hand-crank or battery-powered NOAA weather radio (cell towers may be down;
      this is your information lifeline)
- [ ] Portable phone charger/power bank, fully charged, checked quarterly
- [ ] Whistle (per person — signaling for help without shouting yourself hoarse)
- [ ] Printed list of emergency contacts (phones die; paper doesn't)
- [ ] Local paper map (GPS/data may be unreliable — know your area's routes on paper)

---

## Section 3 — First Aid & Medical

- [ ] Basic first aid kit (bandages, gauze, antiseptic wipes, tape, gloves)
- [ ] 7-day supply of each prescription medication (rotate as prescriptions refill)
- [ ] Any medical devices/supplies unique to your household (inhaler, EpiPen, glucose
      meter + strips, hearing aid batteries)
- [ ] Pain relievers / anti-diarrheal / basic OTC medicine
- [ ] Copy of insurance cards and a list of allergies/conditions per person
- [ ] N95 or dust mask (per person — post-quake dust/debris is a real respiratory risk)

---

## Section 4 — Warmth & Shelter

- [ ] Emergency thermal blanket (compact, per person)
- [ ] A complete change of clothes appropriate for your climate, per person
- [ ] Sturdy closed-toe shoes (you may be walking over broken glass/debris)
- [ ] Rain poncho (compact, per person)
- [ ] Work gloves (heavy-duty — for clearing debris safely)

---

## Section 5 — Cash, Documents & Tools

- [ ] **Cash — small bills.** ATMs and card readers go down with the power grid.
      $100–200 in small bills per household is the standard recommendation.
- [ ] Copies of ID, insurance policies, and important documents in a waterproof bag
      (or securely backed up digitally on a device that doesn't need the internet)
- [ ] Multi-tool or basic tool kit (including the wrench for your gas shutoff — see
      Tool #6)
- [ ] Duct tape and heavy-duty trash bags (repairs, waterproofing, makeshift shelter)
- [ ] Fire extinguisher accessible (not necessarily IN the bag, but staged nearby)

---

## Section 6 — Kids, Pets & Special Needs (add as applicable)

**For kids:**
- [ ] A comfort item (small toy, photo of family) — reduces panic significantly
- [ ] Extra diapers/formula/wipes if applicable, sized for growth (check every 3 months)
- [ ] A card with the child's name, your contact info, and any medical needs (kids
      can be separated from parents in a chaotic evacuation)

**For pets:**
- [ ] 3 days of pet food + collapsible bowl
- [ ] Leash/carrier + copy of vaccination records
- [ ] Pet ID tag with a current phone number

**For elderly/medical-dependent household members:**
- [ ] Extra mobility aid batteries or backup device if power-dependent
- [ ] Written care instructions a helper could follow if you're incapacitated

---

## Section 7 — The Car Kit (separate from the home bags)

Keep this in the trunk — you may be at work or on the road, not at home.

- [ ] Water + non-perishable snacks (smaller quantity, 1-day supply)
- [ ] Flashlight + batteries
- [ ] Emergency blanket + gloves
- [ ] Comfortable walking shoes (if you commute in dress shoes/heels)
- [ ] Small first aid kit
- [ ] Phone charger (car adapter)
- [ ] Printed emergency contact card
- [ ] Cash (small bills)
- [ ] Basic tools + a whistle

## Pro Tips From the Field

1. **Start with water and cash.** If you do nothing else this week, these two items
   cover the two most common post-quake failures: dehydration and "the store won't
   take my card."
2. **Store bags somewhere accessible, not in the attic.** A bag you can't reach
   after a quake (blocked stairs, jammed door) doesn't help you. Near an exterior
   door or in the garage is ideal.
3. **Rotate food/water every 6 months** — tie it to a date you'll remember (daylight
   saving time changes work well as a trigger).
4. **Pack for "grab and go in under 60 seconds."** If building the bag takes 10
   minutes to assemble during an actual emergency, it's not a go-bag — it's a wish list.
5. **Test the weight.** Each family member should be able to carry their own bag for
   at least a mile. Adjust kids' and elderly members' bags accordingly.
