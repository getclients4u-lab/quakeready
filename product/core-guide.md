## Part 1 — What You're Actually Up Against

### The situation in one paragraph

Earthquake clusters are not rare "someday" events — they are a recurring pattern in
every seismically active region, and swarms (multiple small-to-moderate quakes in
days) are often followed by a stronger event or simply repeat for weeks. Most people
who live in quake country have never done a real drop-cover-hold drill, don't know
where their gas shutoff valve is, and have a "go bag" that is really just a backpack
with an old granola bar in it. Meanwhile, the data is consistent everywhere seismologists
study it: **the people who survive with the least injury and the least chaos are the
ones who had already rehearsed the first 90 seconds and prepped the first 72 hours.**
There is no way to predict exactly when a big one hits. There is a completely
proven way to be ready when it does.

### Why "right now" matters

| Factor | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Quake swarms/clusters | Multiple small quakes often precede or accompany the pattern that raises everyone's attention — the exact moment people search for a plan |
| No warning window | Most quakes give 0–10 seconds of P-wave lead time (or a ShakeAlert-style buzz) — your *trained reflex* is the entire plan |
| Aftershock risk | The period after a felt quake carries elevated risk of a second, sometimes larger, event for hours to days |
| Infrastructure exposure | Gas lines, water heaters, unsecured furniture, and un-mounted shelving are the #1 cause of preventable injury — not collapsing buildings |
| Household unpreparedness | Most homes have zero minutes of rehearsed response and under 24 hours of self-sufficient supplies |

### Know your enemy: the three moments that matter

Not all of "earthquake safety" is the same skill. The system you're about to run
targets all three phases, because each one fails differently if you're unprepared:

1. **The shaking itself (0–90 seconds).** This is a reflex problem, not a thinking
   problem. If you have to *decide* what to do while the ground is moving, you've
   already lost several seconds you didn't have. This is trained, not remembered.
2. **The immediate aftermath (first hour).** Gas leaks, damaged utilities, aftershocks,
   and family separation are the highest-injury, highest-panic window. This is a
   checklist problem — you follow steps, you don't improvise.
3. **The following days (72 hours to weeks).** Power outages, water disruption,
   store shortages, and aftershock swarms mean self-sufficiency matters. This is a
   supply and communication-plan problem, solved once and maintained lightly.

**Why this matters:** a fire extinguisher doesn't help you during the shaking, and
knowing "drop, cover, hold on" doesn't help you if your water heater tips and floods
the kitchen with no one knowing how to shut the gas main. The 4-Phase Quake Protocol™
handles all three moments — which is why it works where "just get a go-bag" doesn't.

## Part 2 — The 4-Phase Quake Protocol™: How the System Works

Earthquake readiness happens in four sequential phases. Master each one and your
household moves from "we'll figure it out" to "we already know exactly what to do."

```
        PHASE 4 — RECOVER   (the weekly rhythm that keeps the system alive)
        PHASE 3 — AFTER     (utilities, aftershocks, communication, shelter)
        PHASE 2 — DURING    (drop, cover, hold on — the trained 90-second reflex)
        PHASE 1 — BEFORE    (home hazard removal + go-bag + family plan)
                    YOU  (the protected family, at the center)
```

### Phase 1 — BEFORE: Preparation

**Goal:** remove the hazards that cause injury and build the supplies/plan you'll
need before you ever feel the first jolt.

- Run the **20-Minute Home Hazard Hunt** (Tool #2) once — it finds every tip-over,
  fall, and gas-leak risk in your home.
- Build the **72-Hour Go-Bag** (Tool #3) for every household member, plus one for
  the car. This is the single highest-leverage prep action, full stop.
- Set up your **Family Emergency Plan** (Tool #5) — meeting points, out-of-area
  contact, and the "if we're separated" protocol.
- Locate and label your gas, water, and electrical shutoffs. Know exactly where the
  wrench is (see Tool #6 for the exact shutoff procedure).

**The three preparation mistakes that leave people exposed:**
1. Bolting nothing to the wall (bookshelves, water heaters, TVs — the things that
   tip, fall, or leak are the actual danger, far more than structural collapse).
2. A go-bag with expired water, dead batteries, and no cash (ATMs and card readers
   go down with the power grid).
3. No agreed meeting point or out-of-area contact — kids at school, parents at work,
   phones down, and no plan to reconnect.

### Phase 2 — DURING: The 90-Second Reflex

**Goal:** react correctly in the window you actually have — which is seconds, not minutes.

- Run the **Drop, Cover, Hold On drill** (Tool #4) as a physical, timed family drill
  — not just something you read once. Muscle memory beats memory.
- Know your **location-specific response**: indoors, in bed, outdoors, in a car, in
  a high-rise, at work — each has a specific correct move (all covered in Tool #4
  and Tool #7).
- The rule that does 80% of the work: **Drop where you are. Cover your head/neck
  under sturdy furniture or against an interior wall. Hold on until the shaking
  stops — do not run for a doorway or run outside.**

**Why "drop, cover, hold" beats every myth:** running outside during shaking is how
people get hit by falling debris, glass, and masonry — the leading cause of quake
injury is being struck by falling or flying objects *inside*, not building collapse.
The "triangle of life" and "stand in a doorway" myths persist online and get people
hurt. Tool #4 gives you the evidence-based move for every situation, with no guessing.

### Phase 3 — AFTER: The Critical First Hour

**Goal:** handle the immediate danger — gas, aftershocks, injuries, and communication
— before you do anything else.

- Check yourself and others for injuries first (Tool #6 gives the triage order).
- Check for gas leaks by smell/sound *before* using any switch or open flame; know
  the exact shutoff procedure (Tool #6).
- Expect **aftershocks** — treat every one like the first: drop, cover, hold on again.
- Use text, not calls, to check in with your out-of-area contact (networks jam with
  voice calls; texts get through more reliably).

### Phase 4 — RECOVER: The Weekly Rhythm

**Goal:** make the system automatic so it survives real life — busy weeks, travel,
new house, growing kids — instead of decaying into "we'll get to it."

- The **12-Week Quake-Ready Tracker** (Tool #8) turns the system into a 10-minute-
  per-week habit with a family duty roster.
- Rotate stored water and check expiration dates on food/meds on a schedule, not
  on memory.
- Re-run the **Drop, Cover, Hold On drill** every 90 days — kids grow, routines
  change, and drills fade from memory faster than you think.

## Part 3 — The 7 Rules of Earthquake-Ready Living

Print these. Put them on the fridge. This is the whole system in seven sentences.

1. **Drop, cover, hold on — every time, no exceptions.** Do not run outside, do not
   stand in a doorway, do not try to reach another room.
2. **Secure the tip-overs.** Anchor bookshelves, water heaters, and TVs; the falling
   object is the danger, not the building, in the vast majority of quake injuries.
3. **Build the 72-hour bag before you need it.** Water, food, meds, cash, flashlight,
   radio, first aid — for every person, plus the car.
4. **Know your shutoffs.** Gas, water, electrical — locate them, label them, keep
   the tool nearby, and know the exact procedure (never turn gas back on yourself).
5. **Plan your reconnect, not just your escape.** Meeting point, out-of-area contact,
   and a text-first communication rule beat "we'll just call each other."
6. **Expect the aftershock.** Treat every jolt after the first like a new event —
   drop, cover, hold on again, every single time.
7. **Drill it, don't just read it.** A physical, timed family drill every 90 days
   beats reading this guide once and assuming everyone remembers.

## Part 4 — The First 72 Hours: Your Launch Sequence

You don't need to wait for the weekend. Here is exactly what to do, in order:

**Tonight (20 minutes):**
- [ ] Read the Aftershock & Post-Quake Safety Card (Tool #6) once so you know the
      exact gas-shutoff and injury-triage steps before you ever need them.
- [ ] Do the quick version of the Home Hazard Hunt: walk each room and note every
      unsecured tall/heavy object. Don't fix yet — just count. You'll be surprised.

**Tomorrow (1–2 hours):**
- [ ] Run the full 20-Minute Home Hazard Hunt (Tool #2) room by room.
- [ ] Anchor the top 3 highest-risk items you found (usually: bookshelf, TV, water
      heater strap) or schedule the anchoring this week.
- [ ] Start the 72-Hour Go-Bag (Tool #3) — even a partial bag tonight beats an empty
      closet during the next event.

**This weekend (1 hour):**
- [ ] Finish the Go-Bag for every household member + the car.
- [ ] Fill out the Family Emergency Plan (Tool #5) — meeting points, contacts, the
      out-of-area text-tree.
- [ ] Run the physical Drop, Cover, Hold On drill (Tool #4) as a family, timed.
- [ ] Set up your 12-Week Tracker (Tool #8) and assign the duty roster.

**Done. You are now running the system.** Your household has moved from "we'd
probably panic" to "we already know exactly what to do" — and that shift is the
entire difference between chaos and calm when the ground moves again.
